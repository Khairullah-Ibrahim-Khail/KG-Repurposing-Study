## 3.1 Q1 — KG Augmentation vs. No-KG

**Setup:** identical pretraining for both conditions; the no-KG model uses `depth=0` (no message passing), the KG model uses 2 HGT layers.

**Standard split (indication AUPRC, seeds [42, 0, 1]):**

| Model | AUPRC ind (mean±std) | AUPRC contra (mean±std) |
|-------|---------------------|------------------------|
| gnn_no_kg | 0.893 ± 0.006 | 0.945 ± 0.002 |
| gnn_kg    | 0.825 ± 0.022 | 0.895 ± 0.026 |

**Zero-shot split (indication AUPRC):**

| Model | AUPRC ind (mean±std) | AUPRC contra (mean±std) |
|-------|---------------------|------------------------|
| gnn_no_kg | 0.704 ± 0.027 | 0.725 ± 0.008 |
| gnn_kg    | 0.714 ± 0.026 | 0.831 ± 0.016 |

**Finding:** message passing does not lift indication AUPRC on either split here. On the standard split the no-KG model is clearly ahead. On zero-shot, indication is a wash; the KG model only pulls ahead on contraindication (0.831 vs 0.725) — plausibly because the larger contraindication class benefits from neighborhood smoothing. Reported as-is.

*Source: `results/gnn/{standard,zeroshot}/seed_{42,0,1}/gnn_baseline.json`, `gnn_no_kg.json`*

## 3.2 Q2 — Is Two-Phase Training Beatable?

**Setup:** three regimes on the same TxGNN backbone (64-dim, 2 layers, 4 heads): `txgnn_two_phase` (pretrain then fine-tune), `single_stage` (both losses from epoch 0), `joint_contrastive` (task loss + InfoNCE, no KG pretrain).

**"Better" was fixed in advance:** higher zero-shot indication AUPRC at equal-or-lower wall-clock.

**Zero-shot (indication AUPRC):**

| Method | AUPRC ind | AUPRC contra | Wall-clock (s) |
|--------|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| txgnn_two_phase | 0.736 ± 0.011 | 0.832 ± 0.010 | 71.6 |
| single_stage    | 0.706 ± 0.033 | 0.833 ± 0.019 | 130.7 |
| joint_contrastive | 0.670 ± 0.038 | 0.854 ± 0.016 | 97.1 |

**Standard (indication AUPRC):**

| Method | AUPRC ind | AUPRC contra | Wall-clock (s) |
|--------|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| txgnn_two_phase | 0.816 ± 0.012 | 0.899 ± 0.010 | 183.8 |
| single_stage    | 0.723 ± 0.005 | 0.863 ± 0.012 | 100.3 |
| joint_contrastive | 0.739 ± 0.019 | 0.862 ± 0.014 | 95.9 |

**Finding:** two-phase leads zero-shot indication (0.736 vs 0.706 vs 0.670) and is also the fastest of the three. Joint-contrastive wins zero-shot contraindication (0.854) but trails on indication with high variance. Under the pre-set criterion, two-phase is the winner.

*Source: `results/txgnn/`, `results/alt_single_stage/`, `results/alt_joint_contrastive/`*

## 3.3 Q3 — Why Zero-Shot?

**Conceptual:** Huang et al. (2024) report 92% of PrimeKG diseases have no indication edge. On our download, 9,388 unique indication pairs span 17,080 diseases — consistent with ~8% coverage. A supervised model has nothing to learn from for the rest; disease-similarity transfer is the only route.

**Empirical:** the degradation curve (`results/figures/degradation_curve.png`, data in `results/metrics/degradation_curve_data.json`) compares:
- GNN baseline, standard split — high AUPRC (0.8–0.9) for diseases with many edges, falling toward the zero-edge regime.
- GNN baseline, zero-shot — all test diseases at 0 edges, AUPRC ~0.70 (indication).
- TxGNN, zero-shot — all at 0 edges, AUPRC ~0.74 (indication).

The drop from edge-rich to edge-free diseases is exactly why zero-shot matters; TxGNN's affinity head buys a small but steady edge at 0 training edges.

*Source: `results/figures/degradation_curve.png`, `results/metrics/degradation_curve_data.json`, seed=42.*

## 3.6 Q6 — Is Attention Optional? (It Is Detrimental)

**Rule (set before running):** delta = AUPRC(attn=ON) − AUPRC(attn=OFF) on zero-shot indication. (−0.02, +0.02) → optional; ≥ +0.02 → helps; ≤ −0.02 → hurts.

**Zero-shot ablation table:**

| Variant | AUPRC ind | AUPRC contra | Wall-clock (s) |
|---------|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| txgnn (attn=ON, sim=ON) | 0.736 ± 0.011 | 0.832 ± 0.010 | 71.6 |
| txgnn_no_attn (attn=OFF, sim=ON) | 0.772 ± 0.013 | 0.787 ± 0.008 | 3.2 |
| txgnn_no_sim (attn=ON, sim=OFF) | 0.726 ± 0.007 | 0.824 ± 0.008 | — |
| txgnn_no_both (attn=OFF, sim=OFF) | 0.762 ± 0.010 | 0.780 ± 0.009 | — |

**Decision:** delta = 0.736 − 0.772 = **−0.036** < −0.02 → attention is **detrimental**. Swapping HGT attention for mean aggregation raises zero-shot indication AUPRC by 3.6 points (and runs ~22× faster).

**Reading it:**
- Dropping attention helps — it is not load-bearing, it hurts.
- Dropping similarity (0.726 vs 0.736) hurts slightly — the affinity head does contribute.
- Dropping both (0.762) beats the full model but trails dropping attention alone (0.772) — the two components interact.
- At 64 dims with limited data, the HGT attention weights likely overfit; the published 512-dim model may behave differently.

*Source: `results/ablations/matrix.json`, `results/metrics/q6_ablation_table.csv`*
